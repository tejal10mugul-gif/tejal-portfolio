01 · Executive summary

Make the path from patient population to sales explainable

The workbook is designed to connect an oncology patient population to a commercial sales outlook through epidemiology, disease-progression, eligibility, treatment and sales calculations. The analytical value is traceability: a sales output should be explainable by the patient pool, treatment assumptions and revenue logic behind it.

Clinical / epidemiology

Define the population

Establish the relevant disease population and document diagnosis, stage and eligibility definitions.

Patient conversion

Estimate treatment

Translate the eligible population into treated patients using the model's uptake and treatment assumptions.

Commercial output

Calculate sales

Link treated patients to the model's price and treatment economics, then review the annual trend.

Key questions: How does the modeled disease population translate into a treatable pool? Which assumptions move treated-patient volume? How do those changes flow through to sales?

The workbook contains five principal sheets. The exact formula behavior, source provenance, units and numeric outputs should be confirmed in the file before external use.

03 · Methodology

From epidemiology to a commercial forecast

1

Define the population and time basis

Specify geography, indication, patient definition and forecast period. Distinguish incidence (new cases over a period) from prevalence (people living with a disease at a point or over a period). They are not interchangeable without a defined cohort method.

Document: geography · year · epidemiology measure · unit · source · publication date · adjustment logic.

2

Apply disease and diagnosis logic

Use the disease-progression calculations to estimate the relevant patient pool. State whether the model follows a new-patient cohort, an existing prevalent pool or a combination. Define stages and transitions so patients are not counted twice in the same period.

3

Estimate eligible patients

Apply clinically and commercially relevant filters only when they fit the indication and scope. Depending on the model, these may include diagnosis, stage, biomarker status, line of therapy, contraindications or access. This is a modeled eligible population—not automatically total market size.

4

Estimate treated patients

Translate eligible patients into treated patients using the workbook's uptake or penetration assumptions. If launch timing, duration, persistence, market share or line of therapy are modeled, document their effects on annual counts.

Conceptual relationship: Treated patients = Eligible patients × Treatment penetration

Use this simplified relationship only if the workbook's actual patient definitions and formulas support it. Cohort carryover or treatment duration may require additional terms.

5

Convert treatment into sales

Sales should be traceable to treated patients and the commercial assumptions in the workbook. Depending on the design, the conversion may use treatment duration, dosing, price per unit, units per patient, gross-to-net adjustments or other factors.

Conceptual relationship: Sales = Treated patients × Revenue per treated patient

This is not a substitute for auditing the actual sales formula. Confirm currency, price year, gross/net basis and any treatment or pricing adjustments.

6

Review the annual forecast

Read patient and sales trends together. Once outputs are verified, useful summaries include treated patients by year, annual and cumulative sales, peak sales and peak year, scenario deltas and key sensitivity drivers.

Numerical outputs are intentionally not quoted here. Peak sales, peak year and patient counts should be verified directly in the workbook, including units and price basis, before they are published.
04 · Assumption governance

Make inputs auditable

The Input sheet should be supported by an assumption register with one row per input. Separate externally sourced facts, derived values and illustrative commercial assumptions.

Register fieldWhat to capture
Assumption and definitionClear label; what the variable measures and excludes.
Value and unitValue plus %, patients, years, currency or other unit.
Year and geographyThe period and market to which the value applies.
Source and dateDirect citation or source URL, publication date and access/review date.
Type and rationaleExternal fact, derived value or illustrative assumption; why it is appropriate.
Scenario rangeLow / base / high values where relevant, with rationale.
Review statusValidation owner, review date and status.

If an input is illustrative, label it clearly. Do not present an unsourced workbook value as an industry fact. Clinical evidence, regulatory facts and commercial assumptions should have separate provenance.

05 · Scenario framework

Test uncertainty, not just one forecast

These are recommended scenario definitions, not a claim that scenario analysis is already implemented in the workbook. First verify the base-case formulas and inputs, then test the drivers below.

DriverDownsideBase caseUpside
Diagnosed / eligible populationLower conversionDocumented central assumptionHigher conversion with rationale
Launch timing / accessDelay or slower accessExpected timingFaster access where defensible
Uptake / penetrationSlower rampCentral uptake curveFaster ramp with rationale
Treatment duration / persistenceShorter durationCentral assumptionLonger duration if evidence supports it
Realized priceLower net priceDocumented price basisHigher price only if justified

Start with one-way sensitivity tests on the most material drivers, then consider combined scenarios. Report changes versus base case in absolute and percentage terms. Do not describe scenario outputs as probabilities unless a probability framework has been built.

06 · Quality assurance

Validation checklist before external use

Inputs & formulas

  • Units, time periods, geographies and definitions are consistent.
  • Key outputs trace back to original assumptions.
  • Hard-coded overrides, broken references and formula inconsistencies are identified.
  • Incidence, prevalence and cohort approach are explicit.

Patient flow

  • Counts are non-negative and rates fall within valid bounds.
  • Stage totals and transitions reconcile.
  • No double-counting across stages or years.
  • Eligible and treated patients reconcile to their defined upstream populations.

Commercial outputs

  • Currency, price year, dosing, duration and gross/net basis are explicit.
  • Sales reconcile to treated patients and the actual revenue formula.
  • Launch timing and uptake assumptions align to forecast years.
  • Patient and sales trends are reviewed for unexplained jumps.

Keep a dated record of model version, key assumptions and validation status. Verify peak sales, peak year and cumulative sales from the actual workbook before using those metrics in an interview or public portfolio.

07 · Commercial interpretation

What this framework can help answer

Opportunity sizing

The useful population is the clearly defined eligible pool—not total disease prevalence alone.

Forecast drivers

Separate patient conversion, uptake and revenue-per-patient effects to understand what moves sales.

Launch planning

Explicit launch timing and an uptake curve are more interpretable than an unexplained growth rate.

Evidence priorities

Sensitivity analysis can highlight assumptions needing better epidemiology, clinical, access or pricing evidence.

Governance

A traceable assumption register and formula audit make the forecast easier to review and update.

Recommended summary

After validation, show annual eligible and treated patients, annual and cumulative sales, peak metrics, scenarios and top sensitivities.

Business takeaway: The forecast is a linked chain of clinical and commercial assumptions, not a standalone sales prediction. Its decision value depends on whether the inputs are defensible, the calculations reconcile and the key sensitivities are visible.
08 · Scope and limitations

Transparent about what has—and has not—been validated

  • This page documents the intended analytical logic and a review framework; it does not certify that every control or scenario is implemented in the workbook.
  • Exact formulas, numeric values, source provenance and scenario outputs require verification against the workbook.
  • Do not quote numerical peak-sales or patient outputs until the calculations, units and price basis are checked.
