Oncology Sales Forecasting — Model Notes

Workbook: Oncology Forecasting.xlsx · Forecast horizon described in the portfolio: through 2030

Workbook structure

  • Input: Store epidemiology, population and commercial assumptions with units, source, date and rationale.
  • Calc.1 / Calc. 2: Intermediate patient counts, rates and linked calculations.
  • Cancer Disease Progression: Disease-population and progression logic; stage definitions must be explicit and patient groups should not be double-counted.
  • Forecasting Sheet: Convert the eligible/treated patient pool into projected product sales using commercial assumptions.

Model flow

  1. Define the target population and relevant disease population.
  2. Apply diagnosis, stage/progression and eligibility assumptions.
  3. Calculate treated patients using uptake, access, line-of-therapy and treatment-duration assumptions where applicable.
  4. Translate treated patients into sales using documented price, duration and adjustments.
  5. Review annual trends, peak sales and peak year, then reconcile outputs back to the input and calculation sheets.

Business interpretation

  • Addressable patients should be calculated after diagnosis and eligibility constraints; total prevalence is not the same as commercially treatable population.
  • Treatment penetration, launch timing, duration/persistence and realized price can materially change the sales curve.
  • The model is most useful when peak sales, peak year, treated patients, cumulative sales and sensitivity results are reviewed together.

Validation checklist

  • Add source, source date, unit and rationale to every epidemiology and commercial input.
  • Verify formulas and cross-sheet references; identify hard-coded overrides.
  • Document launch timing, treatment line, eligible-patient definition, forecast cut-off and price basis (gross/net).
  • Add downside, base and upside scenarios plus one-way sensitivity analysis.
  • Check patient counts are non-negative, proportions are valid, patient stages reconcile, and sales reconcile to the patient × price/duration logic.
  • Verify numeric outputs in the workbook before quoting peak sales, peak year or patient counts publicly.
Business conclusion:

The forecast is a linked chain of clinical and commercial assumptions, not a standalone sales prediction. Its decision value comes from showing which assumptions drive the opportunity and how changes in those assumptions alter projected sales.

Independently prepared as commercial and pharma forecasting learning/training work; not a client deliverable. Not for real-world medical, investment or commercial decisions without independent validation.
