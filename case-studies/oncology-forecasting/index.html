01 · Executive overview

Translate clinical and commercial assumptions into a forecast

The model is organized across input, calculation, disease-progression and forecasting sheets. Its central analytical purpose is to make the path from population assumptions to forecast sales visible and auditable.

Clinical logic

Define the relevant population and move through disease progression, diagnosis and eligibility assumptions.

Commercial logic

Translate eligible patients into treated patients using treatment and uptake assumptions.

Financial output

Apply product-level commercial assumptions to derive a sales forecast over the model horizon.

02 · Model architecture

The patient funnel

1
Input assumptions

Population, epidemiology, diagnosis, eligibility, treatment, market penetration and price inputs. Each should be sourced, dated and labelled as an assumption or external fact.

2
Disease progression calculations

Convert the population and disease assumptions into a clinically relevant patient pool, using explicit rates and timing logic.

3
Eligible and treated patients

Apply diagnosis, line-of-therapy, eligibility, uptake and treatment-duration assumptions as appropriate to the indication.

4
Revenue calculation

Link treated patients to price, duration and any relevant adjustments to calculate forecast sales. Clearly define whether outputs are gross sales, net sales or another measure.

5
Forecast outputs

Review annual trend, peak sales and peak year, and reconcile every output back to the assumptions and calculation sheets.

03 · Business conclusions

How to interpret the model for commercial decisions

Core conclusion: The model's value is not a single sales number; it is the ability to show which patient and commercial assumptions drive the forecast, and how changes in those assumptions change the opportunity.
  • Market opportunity: Size the addressable patient pool before applying uptake and price. Avoid equating total disease prevalence with commercially treatable patients.
  • Forecast risk: Diagnosis, eligibility, treatment penetration, duration and price can have different impacts; sensitivity analysis should identify the dominant drivers.
  • Launch planning: A phased uptake curve and explicit launch timing are more decision-useful than a single unqualified growth assumption.
  • Governance: Distinguish epidemiology inputs from commercial assumptions and document whether sales are gross or net.

This page intentionally does not publish numerical peak sales or patient counts because those outputs should be verified against the workbook's calculation results before being presented externally.

04 · Scenario analysis to add

Turn the baseline into a decision tool

Downside

Slower diagnosis, lower eligibility or uptake, delayed launch, shorter treatment duration or lower realized price.

Base case

Documented central assumptions with clear source dates, rationale and forecast cut-off.

Upside

Faster diagnosis, improved access or penetration, stronger persistence or higher eligible-patient conversion, where clinically plausible.

Recommended outputs: peak sales and year, treated patients by year, cumulative sales, scenario deltas, and a sensitivity ranking for the five most material assumptions.

05 · Model documentation

Workbook map and validation checklist

  • Input: Record each assumption, source, date, unit, low/base/high value and rationale.
  • Calc.1 / Calc. 2: Explain each calculation chain and confirm no hard-coded overrides break the flow.
  • Cancer Disease Progression: Define disease stages and progression rates, avoiding double-counting across patient pools.
  • Forecasting Sheet: Document patient-to-sales conversion, price basis, launch timing and forecast period.
  • Quality checks: Check patient counts are non-negative, rates fall within valid ranges, stages reconcile, and formulas are linked consistently.
